The absolute best starting point if you feel lost is the free online book Cherokee Verb Conjugations. Page 13 "Verb structure" and pages 16-17 "Irregularities?" give a great explanation of how a verb works and where to start. It is available online for free here: Cherokee Verb Conjugations

Conjugating a verb means changing part of it to reflect a new situation or meaning. In English, we conjugate verbs, too. It's why we say "you are", "I am", or "s/he or it is." In all these phrases someone "is" something, but the word we use changes.

In Cherokee, we change the beginning of the word to say who is doing the action. The part we change is called the pronoun prefix.

The two main types of pronouns in Cherokee are called the Set A and Set B pronouns. Which set you use depends on the verb and when the action is taking place. This website only shows verbs in the present tense, meaning actions that are happening now. We call a verb that takes a Set A pronoun in the present tense a Set A verb, and we call verbs that use Set B in the present tense Set B verbs.

As you learn the verbs in the game, you will learn if they are Set A or Set B and that will let you know which pronoun to use.

You and I (1st person dual inclusive) ini- in-

We all (1st person plural inclusive) idi- id-
You all (2nd person plural) itsi- its-
They (3rd person plural) ani- an-
